Welcome to the Geodynamics Group at the University of Miami. We combine analytical approaches, large-scale numerical modeling, and geophysical observations to understand how plate and mantle dynamics govern Earth's interior structure and long-term evolution.

The group is based in the Department of Marine Geosciences at the Rosenstiel School of Marine, Atmospheric, and Earth Science and is led by Prof. Adam F. Holt.

Research

Our research focuses on the mechanics and thermal evolution of subduction systems and their role in driving regional and global mantle flow. We develop analytical and computational models to investigate:

  1. Subduction zone mechanics and rheology. How spatial variations in strength, hydration, and evolving material properties influence plate coupling and deformation.
  2. Thermal structure and metamorphic transformation. How slab geometry, convergence rate, and phase changes regulate forearc thermal structure and volatile release.
  3. Tectonic reorganization and plate-boundary interaction. Dynamics of evolving plate systems, including double-subduction configurations and large-scale restructuring.
  4. Mantle circulation and slab-driven flow. Multiscale dynamical models linking subduction forcing to mantle flow structure and long-term tectonic evolution.

Teaching

  • Natural Disasters: Hollywood vs. Reality (GSC 107): Introductory science course on geologic hazards and societal response. Syllabus
  • Geodynamics (MGS 724): Graduate course on rheology, heat and mass transport, and numerical methods. Lecture notes and exercises
  • Subduction Zone Geophysics (MGS 710): Literature-based overview of subduction processes; example topics include slab deformation, mantle flow, rheology, and subduction-induced topography. Syllabus, Schedule
